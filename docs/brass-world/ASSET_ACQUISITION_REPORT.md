## Acquisition options and permissions

1. **Request publisher masters and a written license.** Ask for matching front and reverse production exports in lossless TIFF/PNG or a print PDF with actual trim and color profile information. Request the edition, printing, language, attribution and file handling terms. Ask whether the files can be used as textures in an interactive, browser-delivered 2.5D board for the tournament app, and describe whether gameplay is in scope.
2. **Request permission to capture a verified physical copy.** Use this if Roxley clears the intended use but cannot supply suitable masters. Confirm that the grant covers photography or scanning, geometric and color correction, and any derivatives the project plans to deliver. If a capture service is involved, confirm custody and reuse terms for its deliverables.
3. **Keep public references for inspection.** The official rulebook can help check rules terminology. Product photos and public listings can support provenance inquiries. They do not establish a complete, authorized production source pair.

Ask Roxley to distinguish local evaluation by named developers, private staging or authenticated member access, and public or commercial display. A login wall limits the audience but still delivers copies to browsers. Address hosted textures, caches, backups, screenshots, promotional use, and any downloadable bundle that embeds the artwork. Confirm territory, duration, attribution, edition, fees, and whether separate clearance is needed for branding, commissioned art, or existing digital rights.

Owning the cardboard and taking the photographs do not automatically grant rights to the printed artwork. Private development may fall under a different license or legal exception from redistribution, depending on jurisdiction and use. The project should obtain written scope for each intended use or seek qualified legal review before relying on an exception. Roxley’s website terms are separate from a negotiated board-art license. ## Physical capture workflow

These targets are engineering defaults for this project. Confirm them with a detail sample and adjust to the measured board and smallest printed marks. [FADGI still image digitization guidelines](https://www.digitizationguidelines.gov/guidelines/digitize-technical.html)

1. **Record the specimen and orientation.** Use both faces of the same intact copy. Record publisher, edition, language, SKU/UPC, box or copyright-line evidence of printing, condition, and unfolded trim width and height. Label Day and Night on separate slates outside the artwork. Record top edge and orientation for each face. Photograph packaging and identifiers for provenance where permitted.
2. **Capture flat, sharp and evenly lit.** Prefer a large-format overhead or planetary scanner that fits the board unfolded in one pass. Measure optical resolution at the board plane and disable sharpening, auto contrast, and interpolated DPI. For camera capture, use a rigid copy stand, a low-distortion lens, sensor parallel to the board, fixed focal length and height, RAW, manual white balance and exposure, low ISO, and remote release. Use two matched diffuse lights at roughly 45° and consider cross polarization if gloss hides detail. Keep the fold supported and flat without forcing it or covering printed areas. Record a full-board overview with ruler, neutral gray and color targets in the board plane, with all four corners and a margin visible. Check center and corner focus and avoid clipping highlights or crushing fine marks on the Night face.
3. **Set effective sampling before capturing.** Aim for **300 effective pixels per inch minimum**, with **400 ppi preferred** if the equipment resolves it and the smallest print benefits. Calculate the required pixels from each measured dimension in inches × target ppi. A hypothetical 24 × 18 inch board requires 7,200 × 5,400 pixels at 300 ppi and 9,600 × 7,200 at 400 ppi after cropping. Overlap, margins and perspective correction reduce usable coverage. Measure actual resolved detail with a target. Upscaling does not count toward the target. Recapture if the finest printed text, icons or routes remain ambiguous at 100%.
4. **Capture tiles if a single frame falls short.** Translate the camera or board in a fixed-height grid with approximately 30–40% overlap in both axes. Keep exposure, focus, lighting, orientation and scale locked. Capture adjacent folds in multiple frames. Repeat both the overview and tile grid independently for the second face. A flatbed can serve for individual panels only if they can lie fully flat without stressing the board or leaving an uncaptured strip at its edges.
5. **Calibrate, rectify and stitch.** Develop the RAW files with a consistent profile from the target. Record the per-session white balance, exposure, lens, and ICC profile. Correct radial and tangential distortion using a lens calibration from the same setup before perspective correction. Fit a planar homography from measured corners and/or coplanar targets to the measured trim rectangle. For tiles, register overlaps using feature matches and fixed scale, then check holdout landmarks and fold alignment manually. Place seams in overlaps away from small labels or routes where possible. If a fold lifts out of plane or two tiles disagree, capture again. Keep a record of transforms and seam choices. Preserve the Day/Night tonal differences and use no content-aware filling, tracing, or generated detail.
6. **Check and preserve.** Inspect the full perimeter, folds, and overlaps at native resolution. Confirm no areas are clipped, occluded, duplicated, bent, or blurred. Compare scale and aspect ratio with physical measurements, check small print in every quadrant, and compare color against the captured target. Keep unmodified originals and the color and geometry references, then produce a lossless, color-profiled master per face. Record the capture operator, date, method, equipment and settings, frame IDs, measured dimensions, effective resolution, tile positions, edition and face, transformations, QC reviewer, rights correspondence, and SHA-256 for each original and each master. Store files only in the location and access scope authorized for that stage.
